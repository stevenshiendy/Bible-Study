## 3. "Every place you tread" (Deuteronomy 11:24; Joshua 1:3; cf. Joshua 14:9)

**Timestamps:** [0:52:31] to [0:52:34]; again in the closing prayer [1:33:41] to [1:33:49]

**Use:** At the start he says "Firman Tuhan mengatakan bahwa kemana pun kau pijak sebetulnya itu menjadi milik pusakanya Tuhan". In the closing prayer: "Setiap jengkal tanah yang mereka injak, seperti janji Tuhan kepada Kaleb, ... menjadi milik pusakanya Tuhan."

> **ESV** Deuteronomy 11:24 Every place on which the sole of your foot treads shall be yours. Your territory shall be from the wilderness to the Lebanon, and from the River, the river Euphrates, to the western sea.

> **TB** Ulangan 11:24 Setiap tempat yang diinjak oleh telapak kakimu, kamulah yang akan memilikinya: mulai dari padang gurun sampai gunung Libanon, dan dari sungai itu, yakni sungai Efrat, sampai laut sebelah barat, akan menjadi daerahmu.

> **ESV** Joshua 1:3 Every place that the sole of your foot will tread upon I have given to you, just as I promised to Moses.

> **TB** Yosua 1:3 Setiap tempat yang akan diinjak oleh telapak kakimu Kuberikan kepada kamu, seperti yang telah Kujanjikan kepada Musa.

(Joshua 14:9 and Deuteronomy 1:36, the specific promise to Caleb, are given under entries 6 and 7.)

**Fit check:** The wording exists, but in two forms. The general promise "every place the sole of your foot treads" was given to Israel as a whole, with defined borders (Deut 11:24; Josh 1:3). The promise to Caleb is narrower: "the land on which your foot has trodden" (Josh 14:9; Deut 1:36), meaning the Hebron area he walked through as a spy (Num 13:22). In the sermon opening the general promise is placed inside the Numbers 13 retelling, where it does not occur. In the prayer, "janji Tuhan kepada Kaleb" is right for Josh 14:9, but extending it to "every span of ground" the congregation walks on is an application, not the original scope. "Jengkal" (a hand span) is his wording; TB has "telapak kaki" (sole of the foot). Also the texts say the land becomes Israel's or Caleb's possession ("milikmu", "milik pusakamu"), not "milik pusakanya Tuhan".

**Fit check:**
- **Reading:** Faithful to TB throughout.
- **Gilgal:** He places Gilgal "di dekat Jericho, di atas pegunungan Jericho". Joshua 4:19 puts Gilgal "on the east border of Jericho", in the Jordan valley, which is low ground. "Near Jericho" is right; "on the mountains" is not.
- **45 years:** Joshua 14:10 says exactly "forty-five years" since the word at Kadesh, when Caleb was 40 (14:7), so 85 now. His use at [1:16:44] ("seperti 45 tahun yang lalu") is right. At [1:25:51] he says "Kaleb nunggu 45 tahun susah loh di padang gurun". By Deut 2:14 about 38 of those years were in the wilderness, and the rest (about 7) were spent in the conquest of Canaan. Joshua 14:10's "while Israel walked in the wilderness" covers the main stretch, so "45 years of waiting" is fair, but "45 years in the wilderness" compresses it.
- **"Not talking about physical":** Joshua 14:11 explicitly measures his strength "for war and for going and coming", so it is primarily about physical and military capacity. The inner dimension he draws out (vision, remembering the promise, determination) is well grounded in the repeated "wholly followed" (14:8, 9, 14) and in 14:12's "just as the LORD said". The text supports both, so "not physical" is better taken as "not only physical", which is also how he first put it at [0:56:11] ("bukannya secara fisikal saja").
- **"Maybe it is not written, but God had told him he would defeat the giants in Hebron" ([0:56:45]):** He hedges correctly. Joshua 14:12 has "as the LORD said", but the recorded promises (Num 14:24; Deut 1:36) are about the land for Caleb and his descendants, not explicitly about defeating the Anakim. Joshua 15:14 records that Caleb did drive out the three sons of Anak. Joshua 11:21 credits Joshua with cutting off the Anakim from Hebron, which is a summary statement; the book places the specific clearing of Hebron under Caleb.
- **"Give me that mountain" / "He asked for the mountain God had promised" ([1:27:25], [1:28:45]):** Matches 14:12 ("this hill country of which the LORD spoke", TB "pegunungan").
- **14:15:** "the land had rest from war" fits his point that the fight was still needed before rest.

## 7. Caleb's "different spirit" and the oath: Numbers 14:24, 14:30; Numbers 32:11-12; Deuteronomy 1:36

## 20. Caleb "the Kenizzite": was he ethnically Hebrew?

**Timestamps:** [1:15:14] to [1:15:47]; related [1:29:12] to [1:29:19]

**Use:** "Kaleb itu sebetulnya bukan orang Ibrani. Dia ini orang Keni [Whisper: 'Kenny'] namanya orang Kenas. Dia itu sesuku sama mertuanya Musa sebetulnya. Tetapi dia diadopsi oleh orang Ibrani, dia memilih untuk mengikuti orang Ibrani." He could have left the stubborn Israelites but persevered because he served God's purpose. Later: "Meskipun Kaleb bukan anak kandung Musa, tapi kan Kaleb adalah muridnya Musa."

What the text says about Caleb:

> **ESV** Joshua 14:6 ... Caleb the son of Jephunneh the Kenizzite ... (also 14:14; Numbers 32:12, all quoted above)

> **TB** Yosua 14:6 ... Kaleb bin Yefune, orang Kenas itu ... (also 14:14; Bilangan 32:12)

> **ESV** Numbers 13:6 from the tribe of Judah, Caleb the son of Jephunneh;

> **TB** Bilangan 13:6 dari suku Yehuda: Kaleb bin Yefune;

> **ESV** Numbers 34:19 These are the names of the men: Of the tribe of Judah, Caleb the son of Jephunneh.

> **TB** Bilangan 34:19 Inilah nama orang-orang itu: dari suku Yehuda: Kaleb bin Yefune;

> **ESV** Joshua 15:17 And Othniel the son of Kenaz, the brother of Caleb, captured it. And he gave him Achsah his daughter as wife.

> **TB** Yosua 15:17 Dan Otniel, anak Kenas saudara Kaleb, merebut kota itu; lalu Kaleb memberikan kepadanya Akhsa, anaknya, menjadi isterinya.

> **ESV** 1 Chronicles 4:13 The sons of Kenaz: Othniel and Seraiah; and the sons of Othniel: Hathath and Meonothai. 4:14 Meonothai fathered Ophrah; and Seraiah fathered Joab, the father of Ge-harashim, so-called because they were craftsmen. 4:15 The sons of Caleb the son of Jephunneh: Iru, Elah, and Naam; and the son of Elah: Kenaz.

> **TB** 1 Tawarikh 4:13 Anak-anak Kenas ialah Otniel dan Seraya; dan anak-anak Otniel ialah Hatat dan Meonotai; 4:14 dan Meonotai memperanakkan Ofra. Seraya memperanakkan Yoab, bapa Lembah Tukang-tukang, karena mereka adalah tukang-tukang. 4:15 Anak-anak Kaleb bin Yefune ialah Iru, Ela dan Naam; dan keturunan Ela ialah Kenas.

Where Kenizzites and Kenites appear as peoples:

> **ESV** Genesis 15:19 the land of the Kenites, the Kenizzites, the Kadmonites,

> **TB** Kejadian 15:19 yakni tanah orang Keni, orang Kenas, orang Kadmon,

> **ESV** Genesis 36:11 The sons of Eliphaz were Teman, Omar, Zepho, Gatam, and Kenaz.

> **TB** Kejadian 36:11 Anak-anak Elifas ialah Teman, Omar, Zefo, Gaetam dan Kenas.

Moses' father-in-law:

> **ESV** Exodus 3:1 Now Moses was keeping the flock of his father-in-law, Jethro, the priest of Midian, and he led his flock to the west side of the wilderness and came to Horeb, the mountain of God.

> **TB** Keluaran 3:1 Adapun Musa, ia biasa menggembalakan kambing domba Yitro, mertuanya, imam di Midian. Sekali, ketika ia menggiring kambing domba itu ke seberang padang gurun, sampailah ia ke gunung Allah, yakni gunung Horeb.

> **ESV** Numbers 10:29 And Moses said to Hobab the son of Reuel the Midianite, Moses' father-in-law, "We are setting out for the place of which the LORD said, 'I will give it to you.' Come with us, and we will do good to you, for the LORD has promised good to Israel."

> **TB** Bilangan 10:29 Lalu berkatalah Musa kepada Hobab anak Rehuel orang Midian, mertua Musa: "Kami berangkat ke tempat yang dimaksud TUHAN ketika Ia berfirman: Aku akan memberikannya kepadamu. Sebab itu ikutlah bersama-sama dengan kami, maka kami akan berbuat baik kepadamu, sebab TUHAN telah menjanjikan yang baik tentang Israel."

> **ESV** Judges 1:16 And the descendants of the Kenite, Moses' father-in-law, went up with the people of Judah from the city of palms into the wilderness of Judah, which lies in the Negeb near Arad, and they went and settled with the people.

> **TB** Hakim-hakim 1:16 Keturunan Hobab, ipar Musa, orang Keni itu, maju bersama-sama dengan bani Yehuda dari kota pohon korma ke padang gurun Yehuda di Tanah Negeb dekat Arad; lalu mereka menetap di antara penduduk di sana.

> **ESV** Judges 4:11 Now Heber the Kenite had separated from the Kenites, the descendants of Hobab the father-in-law of Moses, and had pitched his tent as far away as the oak in Zaanannim, which is near Kedesh.

> **TB** Hakim-hakim 4:11 Adapun Heber, orang Keni itu, telah memisahkan diri dari suku Keni, dari anak-anak Hobab ipar Musa, dan telah berpindah-pindah memasang kemahnya sampai ke pohon tarbantin di Zaanaim yang dekat Kedesh.

**Fit check:**
- **What the text supports.** Caleb is "the Kenizzite" (Hebrew *haqqenizzi*, TB "orang Kenas") in Num 32:12 and Josh 14:6, 14. The Kenizzites appear in Gen 15:19 as one of the peoples of the land promised to Abraham, and Kenaz is also an Edomite clan name (Gen 36:11, 15, 42). Yet Caleb is also the chosen leader of the tribe of Judah (Num 13:6; 34:19), receives his inheritance "among the people of Judah" (Josh 15:13), and appears in Judah's genealogy (1 Chr 4:13-15). Many commentators therefore conclude that Caleb's clan was of Kenizzite (non-Israelite, possibly Edomite-related) origin and was absorbed into Judah. So "originally not of Israelite stock, incorporated into Judah" is a reasonable and widely held inference, though Scripture never says in so many words that he was "not Hebrew" or "adopted".
- **Where care is needed.** Moses' father-in-law was a Midianite (Exod 3:1; Num 10:29) and his clan is called "the Kenite" (Judg 1:16; 4:11), TB "orang Keni". Kenites (Keni) and Kenizzites (Kenas) are two different groups, listed side by side in Gen 15:19. So "sesuku sama mertuanya Musa" (same tribe as Moses' father-in-law) is not supported: it confuses Keni with Kenas. The transcript shows the slip itself: "orang Keni namanya orang Kenas". (Both groups did end up attached to Judah, Judg 1:16, which may be why they are easily merged.)
- **Translation note.** In Judg 1:16 and 4:11 TB calls Hobab "ipar Musa" (brother-in-law), whereas ESV has "father-in-law". The Hebrew word can cover either relation by marriage, and Num 10:29 calls Hobab the son of Reuel (Jethro). This does not affect the Keni/Kenas point.
- **"Muridnya Musa".** Scripture does not call Caleb Moses' disciple or assistant; that title belongs to Joshua (Exod 24:13; 33:11; Num 11:28). Caleb was sent by Moses (Josh 14:7) and served under him, so "a man formed under Moses" is fair as illustration, but "murid" is the preacher's term.

## Points needing care

1. **Kenite versus Kenizzite (entry 20).** Caleb is "the Kenizzite" (Kenas); Moses' father-in-law was a Midianite called "the Kenite" (Keni). These are different groups (Gen 15:19 lists them separately). "Sesuku sama mertuanya Musa" is not supported. "Originally not Israelite, absorbed into Judah" is a reasonable scholarly inference, but Scripture also presents Caleb as Judah's own tribal leader (Num 13:6; 34:19) and never says he was "adopted".
2. **Order of events in 1 Kings 19 (entry 16).** In the wilderness God feeds Elijah through an angel; the question "What are you doing here, Elijah?" comes at Horeb, after the 40-day journey (19:9, 13). God does not send him to Horeb; he goes there strengthened by the food.
3. **"45 years in the wilderness" (entries 6, 4).** Joshua 14:10's 45 years run from Kadesh to the day Caleb speaks: about 38 in the wilderness (Deut 2:14) plus roughly 7 in the conquest. "45 years since the promise" is exact; "45 years in the wilderness" compresses it.
4. **"Not talking about physical" (entry 6).** Joshua 14:11 does speak of physical strength "for war and for going and coming". The inner strength he describes is well grounded in "wholly followed", so it is best stated as "not only physical".
5. **"Going out and coming in" (entry 23).** The idiom means active leadership and warfare (Num 27:17; Deut 31:2; 1 Kgs 3:7). The tabernacle sense is a devotional application, not a meaning of Joshua 14:11.
6. **"Every place your foot treads" (entry 3).** The general promise (Deut 11:24; Josh 1:3) was to Israel within set borders; Caleb's promise (Josh 14:9; Deut 1:36) was the land he walked as a spy. Applying it to "every span of ground" the congregation steps on is an application.
7. **Deuteronomy 34:7 in The Message (entry 14).** "Spring in his step" is the MSG's paraphrase of "his vigor unabated"; and "his eye was undimmed" is physical eyesight, which he reads spiritually. Deut 31:2 (Moses can "no longer go out and come in") is a useful counterbalance.
8. **Gilgal (entry 6).** Gilgal is on the east border of Jericho in the Jordan valley (Josh 4:19), not "di atas pegunungan Jericho".
9. **Smaller items.** Two men carried the grapes, not "several" (Num 13:23). Caleb is not called Moses' disciple; Joshua is Moses' assistant (entry 20). Hermon is the preacher's contrast, not in Joshua 14 (entry 24). "Mata adalah jendela hati" is a proverb, not a verse (entry 9). Psalm 37:23 concerns the course of life, not gait (entry 15). Uriah is not named in 2 Sam 11:1 (entry 19). Deut 28:13 is conditional on obedience (entry 27).
10. **Quoted versions confirmed.** Psalm 34:5 is quoted word for word from the NIV (TB 34:6). Deuteronomy 34:7 is quoted from The Message. "Why do you look so sad?" (Gen 40:7) is NIV wording. Nehemiah 8:10 is TB 8:11, where TB has "perlindunganmu" rather than "kekuatanmu".
