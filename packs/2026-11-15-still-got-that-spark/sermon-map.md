# Understanding: "Still Got That Spark" (Masih Nyala)

- Service: IFGF Bandung Sunday Service (first service, "ibadah yang pertama"), 15 November 2026
- Preacher: Pastor Sam (named by the hosts at [1:34:29])
- November series: "Dreams and Visions" (mimpi dan penglihatan)
- Main text: Joshua 14:6-14, with back story from Numbers 13 to 14
- Sermon span in the transcript: [0:51:28] to [1:34:24]

---

## 1. Summary

Pastor Sam opens the "Dreams and Visions" series by saying that God gives dreams and visions throughout Scripture, still gives them today, but that believers should not wait for dreams, prophecies or signs before acting: they are called to follow God, and the Bible already tells them what to do. He tells the story of the twelve spies and the forty years in the wilderness, then reads Joshua 14:6-14, where Caleb at 85 says he is as strong as he was at 40 and asks for the hill country of Hebron that God promised. He stresses that what set Caleb apart was not his desire for land but that he "followed the Lord wholeheartedly" and had "a different spirit," and that his "strength" at 85 means strength of vision, memory of God's promise and determination, not only physical strength. He then gives three qualities every Christian should have, each tied to a "vision" tagline: Excitement ("Vision awakens our passion"), Expectation ("Vision sustains our resilience"), and Experience ("Vision inspires our participation"). He illustrates these with Scripture (Psalm 34, 2 Corinthians 3, Moses' "spring in his step," Elijah, Joseph, David, Psalm 27) and with personal stories (his father's Wednesday house church and daily sermon writing until his death, his son Nathan's proposal, holidays with his wife, family meals, hiking so he can later hike with grandchildren). He insists that excitement alone is not enough, that expectation is not presumption, and that nothing happens unless it is acted on; Caleb received his promise and Moses did not, which shows that God's promises cross generations and that the journey with God matters more than the reward. He closes with three take-home questions and a prayer.

**Central claim (one sentence):** A God-given vision keeps a believer's spark alive at every age by awakening real excitement, sustaining expectation that trusts God's character, and moving him to act, as Caleb did when at 85 he still asked for the mountain God had promised.

## 2. Outline with timestamps

### [0:51:28] Introduction to the series
- Transition from announcements (a joke at [0:51:11] that parking at "Pascal" sometimes requires "dreams and visions," so come early).
- The theme this month is dreams and visions. God speaks of them throughout Scripture and gives them not only to the spiritually senior but to a young boy, Joseph, and to a very wise man, Daniel. The New Testament has a whole book devoted to it, Revelation (Wahyu): "visions from God, revelations from God."

### [0:52:16] Back story: Numbers 13 to 14
- In Numbers, Moses sent twelve spies (pengintai) into the promised land to find out what it contained. He adds that "wherever you set your foot" becomes God's inheritance (milik pusaka).
- Two of the twelve, Joshua and Caleb, brought a good report: a land flowing with milk and honey, fertile and good, which God had given. The other ten agreed the land was good (they even carried a cluster of grapes so large it took several men), but said there were giants and fortified cities.
- He notes they were people just out of slavery, so it was natural to be intimidated. The ten said "we can't"; Joshua and Caleb said "we can."
- God was angry at Israel's unbelief; they wandered about 38 years, then about two years of preparation, 40 years in the wilderness in all.
- When the time came again, they sent spies again, saw how much God would do before them, and entered across the Jordan. Briefly from the book of Joshua: Joshua attacked from various sides, and they conquered almost all of it, because Scripture says they did not finish the conquest. Then the land was divided as inheritance.

### [0:54:20] The reading: Joshua 14:6-14 (Indonesian TB wording, with commentary)
- Reads v.6 onward: the people of Judah (Bani Yehuda) came to Joshua at Gilgal, which he explains was their encampment center, near Jericho.
- Reads Caleb's words: he was 40 when Moses sent him from Kadesh Barnea; he brought back an honest report; his brothers made the people's hearts melt; "I followed the LORD my God wholeheartedly."
- Paraphrases Moses' oath: "Caleb, because you brought good news, you will be given a reward": the land your feet have walked on will be your inheritance and your children's forever.
- Comment at [0:55:26]: of the millions who could enter the land, only two of the old generation did, Joshua and Caleb.
- Reads vv.10-11: God has kept me alive 45 years; I am 85 today; I am as strong now as when Moses sent me, for war and for "going out and coming in."
- Comment at [0:56:06]: his "strength" is not only physical; he was equally strong in vision, in remembering God's promise, and in determination to fulfil God's call. "It's not talking about physical."
- Reads v.12: give me this hill country; the Anakim are there with great fortified cities; perhaps the LORD will be with me and I will drive them out. Comment: "maybe it is not written," but it turns out God had already told him that Caleb would be able to defeat the giants of Hebron, so Caleb says, "I want to see that happen; even at 85 I still want to defeat giants."
- Reads vv.13-14: Joshua blessed Caleb and gave him Hebron as his inheritance "to this day," because he wholly followed the LORD.
- Comment at [0:57:18]: "I don't know why he insisted on Hebron." Hebron is still very special to Jews: the burial place of Abraham, Sarah, Isaac and Jacob in the cave of Machpelah. Caleb says, "I want Hebron, I remember God's promise, I will defeat those giants."

### [0:57:37] The title and the image of a "spark"
- Title: "Still got that spark," rendered "masih nyala" (still alight), and also "masih ngotot, masih mau, masih ingin" (still insistent, still willing, still wanting).
- The easiest way to see a spark is in the eyes. Some eyes are blazing, look happy, excited. The eyes are "the window of the heart": you can tell if someone is enthusiastic, passionate, full of aspirations.
- People often do the same activities, but you can tell from body language, eyes and way of speaking whether they have spark, and whether they bring a positive atmosphere into a room. Some people walk into a room and it is charged up, alight; others walk in and the room turns gloomy, as if a black cloud hung in the middle.
- Many people are still working, still going to church, still active Monday to Saturday, still leaving the house, still meeting people, but is something missing? Is there still a spark, a flame, a fire?

### [0:59:21] God still gives dreams and visions, but do not be hyper-spiritual: follow God and the Bible
- Throughout Scripture God gave direction by dreams and visions: to Jacob, Joseph, even Paul; a command to Jonah; visions to Ezekiel, Isaiah, Jeremiah.
- Is there still prophecy today? Yes. Can God still give dreams and visions? That is His promise in Joel and Acts: old and young will receive visions and dreams.
- But we do not always have to wait for a dream, or for a prophet to prophesy over us, before doing something; we do not need sign after sign ("if the sky is pink tomorrow, then it is from God").
- God still gives visions and dreams, but we now have the Bible, God's word, where we can read God's visions and dreams every day, because the Bible is complete.
- "I don't want you all to be too hyper-spiritual," needing an audible voice before moving. "I don't think God works like that."
- "We are called to follow God, and not dreams and visions." Dreams and visions are good, but if there are none yet, there is the Bible: what it says you may do, do; what it says you may not do, do not. "Very simple." As we walk in that, dreams and visions will realize themselves, as we do what we already know of God.

### [1:01:24] What set Caleb apart
- Read Numbers at home: Caleb's focus was not the land. Yes, he insisted on Hebron; that may have been his dream and aspiration, nothing wrong with that.
- But Numbers clearly says what distinguished Caleb from millions of others and from the ten spies: "he followed the Lord wholeheartedly"; he had "a different spirit," a different heart.
- So at 85 he says "let's go forward, let's go in," just as at 40 he said "we can, we can."

### [1:02:14] Three qualities ("three entities, three traits, three characters") every Christian should have

#### Point 1 [1:02:26] Excitement: "Vision awakens our passion"
- Excitement is important. It is not only for sanguine people, extroverts or introverts; God gives excitement to everyone regardless of personality type. We box ourselves in ("I'm an introvert, I'm an extrovert"), but this is something general in Scripture.
- [1:03:00] Tagline: "Vision awakens our passion": vision from God awakens our passion (gairah) in Him. Definition: joy, cheerfulness, strong feeling of happiness, enthusiasm, even a degree of commotion and excitement.
- [1:03:22] Illustration: Ruth, today's worship leader (WL), is full of excitement; at the office everyone knows when she is coming because she shouts from the ground floor (the shouted word is unclear in the transcript).
- [1:03:46] For someone close to God who receives a vision, excitement is not merely an emotional response to opportunity ("I can get Hebron, I can be blessed, I can get a promotion"). Nothing wrong with that, but it is not only that. Caleb knew there was a call of God there: "it's not just a city," not just a promotion. He pursued God's call. Why? Because following God's call requires staying close to God; otherwise people fall into a "default mode" and follow their own instincts and their own call.
- [1:04:42] Illustration: it is great to travel with someone full of excitement: "come on, come on, don't just stay at the hotel, get out of the car," even when it is hot or raining. Not only when things are happy; such a person brings something new.
- [1:05:11] Illustration (comic role play): imagine if he came every Sunday without excitement: "Guys, happy Sunday... here we go again, another Sunday... why does Sunday come so fast... thanks for coming... I don't know what I'll preach today but I'll try... Why am I here?"
- [1:05:48] Psalm 122:1, "I was glad when they said to me, let us go to the house of the LORD." Even Scripture has an element of excitement; as if to say, "If you're not glad, why come to the house of the Lord?"
- [1:06:22] When people understand dreams and vision in their lives, their face changes, regardless of being extrovert or introvert. Psalm 34:5 (read in English): "Those who look to him are radiant; their faces are never covered with shame." Nothing to do with being sanguine; you do not need to be quiet or talkative, but you do not hang your head before people. If you can kneel before God, you need not fear anyone.
- [1:07:22] 2 Corinthians 3:16-18: when a heart turns to the Lord the veil is removed; the Lord is the Spirit; where the Spirit of the Lord is, there is freedom; we all reflect the Lord's glory. You can see from a face who is free. "I am not saying act cheerful (sok riang). No, no, no. Not from outside in, but from inside out." Where there is freedom, there is joy, peace, excitement.
- [1:08:18] Congregation exercise: ask left and right, "Are you excited?" He prays nobody goes home from the office thinking "I have to go home," or goes to the office thinking "I have to work." When playing with your children, really have fun and enjoy it. Turning to God shows outwardly whatever your personality.
- [1:09:02] Deuteronomy 34:7 read from The Message: Moses at 120, when he died, could still see (he says: still see God, still see God's vision and dream, even though he could not enter the land); "he still walked with a spring in his step," as if he had springs in his feet. Moses did not drag his feet.
- [1:09:55] Illustration: he is most irritated seeing young people drag their feet ("your life seems so heavy, so young already"). He often tells restaurant waiters (his wife does not know, since she dislikes him correcting people): if you want a long career in any field, do not drag your feet when you walk. Joke: "Scripture says the drag (seretan) of the righteous is led by the Lord... no: the steps (langkah) of the righteous are led by the Lord." He tells his own child: do not drag your feet, you have a spine, stand upright. (Congregation told to say to their neighbours; "he's not fierce, he's just like that.")
- [1:11:01] Elijah: when Elijah was sulking (pundung, then restated as murung for out-of-towners), offended at God, far from God, God asked: why no excitement? Why go all the way to the wilderness and sit here wanting to die? "What are you doing here?" God saw no passion. "Go." He went to Mount Horeb, walking 40 days.
- [1:11:33] Joseph in prison (from a sermon a few weeks earlier): the cupbearer and baker were sad; Joseph, also in prison but with joy, asked them, "Why do you look so sad?"
- [1:11:50] David: young David was full of excitement and passion. As a boy, facing Goliath, though it was not his job, he asked, "Who is this uncircumcised man who defies the armies of the living God?" He took a stone, killed Goliath, and cut off his head. But when old, at the time when kings go to war, he had no excitement: he told Joab and Uriah to go attack the Ammonite capital, Rabbah, while he stayed in Jerusalem. Nothing to pursue, everything ordinary; in his boredom he went up to his roof, saw a woman bathing, and fell into sin. People without excitement get bored, and "nothing matters anymore."
- [1:13:00] Joke: be excited even ordering food in a restaurant ("What's this, miss? What's this?") until the waitress wonders, "Is this guy hungry or broken?" Then an aside to online viewers: "sorry, that was meant for the second service."
- [1:13:22] For God's people, healthy excitement comes from doing ordinary daily routines (waking, breakfast, going to the office, serving our families) filled with God's great purpose: "I am not just doing this and that; I am serving the visions and dreams God has given."
- [1:14:12] Congregation says: "Fill yourself with excitement," "Be excited about life." This is not self-hypnosis ("positive, positive"). "No, no, no": it is from inside out, from understanding God's purpose and being guided by God's word; from that come vision and planning.

#### Point 2 [1:14:38] Expectation: "Vision sustains our resilience"
- Without excitement a person has no expectation; or if he has hope he says it can no longer be fulfilled. He can make big visions but say, "Could that really happen?"
- [1:14:58] Tagline: "Vision sustains our resilience": vision supports and upholds our tenacity (kegigihan). We are tenacious not only because we are diligent but because there is "the purpose of God."
- [1:15:14] Caleb was "actually not a Hebrew"; he was a Kenizzite (he also says "Kenite"), "of the same tribe as Moses' father-in-law," adopted by the Hebrews, who chose to follow them. He could have said, "These people are all stubborn, they won't enter the land," and just left. But he persevered because "I am not only serving you; I am serving God's purpose in my life."
- [1:15:49] When we see God we want to do something. We need excitement and passion, but "excitement alone is not enough." Many people are "cheerful, cheerful, cheerful" but have no expectation, cannot write a plan, cannot organize, their own calendar is a mess, schedules exist but are scattered. Very positive, full of faith, but their expectation cannot be seen or read by others.
- [1:16:39] But Caleb said, "I am still strong," as 45 years before: at 85 still expectant, still full of hope, still full of vision, still tenacious.
- [1:16:56] Tagline: "Expectation is what keeps yesterday's promise relevant to today's obedience." Indonesian: expectation makes the promise we received yesterday still connect if we want to obey today. We obey God's promise received in the past, and while the promise is not yet fulfilled we must still obey today, because we are full of expectation that God will do great things.
- [1:17:34] Caleb teaches why I must keep fighting: because I will see God's promise fulfilled, I will see that God is good.
- [1:17:45] Psalm 27:13-14: "I believe I will see the goodness of the LORD in the land of the living." Whether we actually see it is God's business, but expectation must not drop. Moses did not see it; he saw from afar and did not enter. Every believer, while alive, must have the expectation, "I will see God's goodness while I live." "Wait for the LORD; be strong and take heart."
- [1:18:26] Illustration, his father's house church: his father came to faith (bertobat) at 50. A few months later he opened his home for worship. On Sundays many young people came early to set up chairs, but on Wednesdays, for 25 years until he was 75, his father set up the chairs himself. (Joke: "Don't look at me like that, I was at school till 3." "Ungrateful son, didn't help.") From 1 or 2 pm, full of excitement and expectation, he set up chairs; when the son came home his father was praying over the chairs and over people's names. From Monday he phoned people, from Tuesday he visited all the neighbours. He always said, "Someone will surely be baptized, someone will surely believe." At first the son did not believe it, but he saw neighbours, relatives and kin one by one receive Jesus and be baptized.
- [1:20:01] Later, retired, at 75, unable to go anywhere (he died at 86), his father knew his son ministered in many places and always said, "Surely many will repent, many will be baptized." He could not go, but he passed his excitement on.
- [1:20:23] A few weeks ago, clearing his parents' house after his mother died, he found his father's sermon notes: up to two weeks before his death his father wrote a sermon almost every day, as if the Wednesday service were still running and he would preach every week. When he phoned, he would say, "Papa got a great sermon," tell it at length, and ask the son to preach it if he had the chance. "Still writing sermons" before he died. Prayer that we glorify God to our last breath.
- [1:21:32] The Adele joke: do not listen too often to Adele, though it is nice: "This is the end... close your eyes and count to ten," while you are still young, 30 or 40. "Don't be like that."
- [1:21:53] Congregation says: "Expect something." Family life must be full of hope.
- [1:22:02] Illustration, Nathan's proposal: his son Nathan proposed to his fiancee yesterday (he has permission to show the photo). Nathan was full of excitement; he left the house at 5 am to surprise her with "Will you marry me" ("kids these days; in my day it was very simple"). The father could not sleep or eat; he prayed, in tongues (bahasa roh); "I confess, I didn't pray that long even for today's sermon." Nathan was excited and so was he. But life is not enough with excitement only; there must be expectation, for them, that God will be with them every day, from now to the wedding day and beyond. Some people only want the excitement ("we're honeymooning, we're married, we're engaged"). What is the expectation? It must be wrapped in prayer.
- [1:23:41] Illustration, holidays with his wife: not only excitement (going, playing, travelling) but the expectation that "we want to grow old together, grow young together, grow mature together." Otherwise it is only a trip, which anyone can take.
- [1:24:17] Illustration, family meals: gathering working adult children is hard, but they make time. It is not only the meal and the excitement, but the expectation: what if at the table we can impart something good?
- [1:24:40] Illustration, hiking: he walks at Sabuga three times a week and goes hiking; he enjoys it, but the expectation is to be strong enough to take his grandchildren hiking later, rather than being the grandpa (kungkung) who is tired, lazy, worried about rain or heat, bored. "If you're like that, it's up to you," but he walks to be healthy and to take his grandchildren to (safe) places.
- [1:25:36] In ministry likewise, not "whatever, as long as I lead a church": there must be expectation that people receive Jesus, are baptized, restored, healed.
- [1:25:51] Caleb waited 45 years, hard, in the wilderness, but proved that "God is faithful."
- [1:25:57] Tagline: "Expectation is not assuming God will fulfill every dream. It is trusting his character while pursuing purposes in obedience." Indonesian: expectation does not mean "because I hope in God, God is obliged to fulfill all my dreams"; it is trusting God's character while pursuing God's calling with obedience.
- [1:26:22] Some dreams are fulfilled, as with Caleb; some are not, as with Moses. "That's fine." "Isn't the journey more important than the reward?"

#### Point 3 [1:26:32] Experience: "Vision inspires our participation"
- Caleb had excitement and expectation; "only then will he undergo experience." Tagline: "Vision inspires our participation": he experienced it, he did it.
- [1:26:50] At 85 he could still "go out and come in." This has two meanings: going out (to battle, beyond the city) and also going in and out of the tabernacle. He could still pray and still fight.
- [1:27:09] However big the excitement or expectation, it will not happen unless it is worked on. There must be experience, our participation (peran serta).
- [1:27:25] "Give me that mountain": he knew that afterwards he had to fight; he knew there was God's promise but he had to defeat the giants, the Anakim; he had to work and struggle. Dreams, vision, zeal and big expectation are all good, "but they will not happen if they are not done."
- [1:27:50] Someone who walks with God, enjoys being near Him, has expectation in God and is willing to act, will not envy others' blessings. Caleb did not ask for another mountain someone else might have, nor the highest mountain, Hermon; he did not want another valley or a river. "If God gives me Hebron, that's more than enough; what matters is that I experience the journey with God." He was secure in God: never mind who has that mountain or the one over there; "I was given Hebron and I will fight for Hebron."
- [1:28:45] Tagline: "He asked for the mountain God had promised." Make sure you pursue the right mountain, something that is truly your portion in this life.
- [1:28:59] Caleb got it; Moses did not; "it's okay." What matters is seeing God, walking with God and experiencing God.
- [1:29:09] Promises cross generations: though Caleb was not Moses' biological son, he was Moses' disciple. Moses did not receive it, Caleb did. "God's promises cross generations." If you are full of excitement, pure expectation, and do whatever needs doing, then if this generation does not receive it, the next will, "in Jesus' name."
- [1:29:43] What inheritance do they get? Not only "this and that" (nothing wrong with that) but let us pass on excitement in the Lord: say to our children, "I was glad when they said, let us go to the house of the Lord"; "Papa is going to work, pray for me: today is gonna be a great day," and the child says it too. Expectation: teach them to pray "Lord, if you are willing (jika engkau berkenan)": open this door if you allow it, close it if you do not. And teach them to fight, to do whatever is needed. That is an important inheritance.
- [1:30:51] Tagline: "Recognize God's work through your experiences," rendered "Mengenali rancangan Tuhan melalui pengalaman-pengalaman kehidupan kita."

### [1:31:01] Closing reflection questions
1. "What still ignites my passion for God?" ("Apa sih yang masih membuat saya menyala?") He prays they are still on fire for family and for God.
2. "What am I looking forward to with faith?" ("Apa sih yang masih saya harapkan, yang masih saya doakan, yang masih saya punya ekspektasi yang besar?")
3. (Given in Indonesian only) "Langkah iman apa yang saya siap untuk ambil?" (What step of faith am I ready to take?)

### [1:31:35] Response song and closing prayer
- Congregation stands; "How Great Is Our God."
- [1:32:33] Prayer guide: ask God to remind you to have excitement in life, passion, joy in the Lord; to have expectation again if it has been lost; and to experience God in every step, "because my greatest adventure is when I'm walking with you, when I experience you."
- [1:33:11] Prayer: we need excitement that is not from outside as happiness (nothing wrong with that) but from inside out, because "from within you will flow rivers of living water." May God's house rise, be the head and not the tail, succeed in everything, be blessed in family, work, business, school. Every bit of ground they tread becomes their inheritance, as God promised Caleb. But not only receiving by faith and having expectation: "we must do it, because faith without works is useless." Bless every family and marriage with "fresh excitement" from God, so that their children see that the joy of the Lord is their strength. In the name of Jesus. Amen.

## 4. The argument as a chain of reasons

### Step A: Framing the series (dreams and visions)
- Premise: God gives dreams and visions throughout Scripture and still does (Joel / Acts promise).
- Qualification: but we have the complete Bible, which is God's vision for us every day.
- Conclusion: believers are called to follow God, not to chase dreams and signs; obey what the Bible already says, and dreams and visions will "realize themselves" as we walk in obedience.
- Why this matters for the rest: it sets "vision" as something tied to God's purpose and God's word, not private experiences. Every later point (excitement "from inside out," expectation "in obedience," participation) assumes this.

### Step B: What made Caleb different
- Premise: Caleb asked for Hebron at 85, but Numbers says the distinguishing mark was that he followed the Lord wholeheartedly and had a different spirit.
- Why it follows: his desire for land was legitimate, but it flowed from his following God, not the reverse.
- Conclusion: his "strength" at 85 is strength of vision, memory of God's promise, and determination. The spark is the inner fruit of following God, and it does not fade with age.

### Step C: The three E's, each built on the one before
1. **Excitement** (Vision awakens our passion)
   - Premise: a vision from God awakens passion in anyone, whatever their personality.
   - Distinction: true excitement is not just an emotional reaction to opportunity (city, blessing, promotion); it responds to God's call, which keeps a person close to God instead of in "default mode" following his own instincts.
   - Evidence: Psalm 122:1, Psalm 34:5, 2 Corinthians 3:16-18 (turning to God brings freedom that shows on the face), Moses' "spring in his step."
   - Negative examples: Elijah without excitement asked "what are you doing here?"; David, bored in later life, fell into sin. So the absence of excitement is dangerous, not neutral.
   - Conclusion: excitement must come from inside out, from understanding God's purpose and being guided by His word, and from that come vision and planning.
2. **Expectation** (Vision sustains our resilience)
   - Link from point 1: "If a person has no excitement, he has no expectation." Excitement is the precondition. But "excitement alone is not enough": cheerful people who cannot plan or schedule have no visible expectation.
   - Premise: vision sustains tenacity because it rests on God's purpose, not diligence. Caleb, an outsider by birth (as the preacher describes him), could have left stubborn Israel, but he served God's purpose.
   - Key definition: expectation keeps yesterday's promise relevant to today's obedience. While a promise is unfulfilled, we still obey today because we expect God to do great things.
   - Guardrail: expectation is not presumption ("God must fulfill all my dreams"); it is trusting God's character while pursuing His purpose in obedience.
   - Evidence: Psalm 27:13-14, the father's house church and sermon writing, Nathan's proposal, marriage holidays, family meals, hiking for future grandchildren, expectation in ministry.
   - Conclusion: whether we see the fulfilment is God's business, but expectation must not drop.
3. **Experience** (Vision inspires our participation)
   - Link from point 2: "He had excitement, he had expectation; only then will he undergo experience." However large excitement or expectation is, "it will not happen if it is not worked on."
   - Premise: Caleb said "give me that mountain" knowing he must then fight the Anakim. Promise and effort go together. The closing prayer restates this: "faith without works is useless."
   - "Going out and coming in" covers both battle and the tabernacle: he could still fight and still pray, so participation includes both action and devotion.
   - Conclusion: experience is our participation, and through it we recognize God's work (rancangan) in our lives.

### Step D: Moses and Caleb together
- Moses did not enter the land; he saw it from afar. Yet he still "walked with a spring in his step" (excitement) and is the example that expectation does not depend on seeing the outcome personally.
- Caleb did enter and received Hebron, proving "God is faithful."
- Combined conclusion 1 (about expectation): some dreams are fulfilled (Caleb), some are not (Moses), "that's fine," because expectation trusts God's character rather than guaranteeing outcomes, and the journey matters more than the reward.
- Combined conclusion 2 (about generations): Caleb was not Moses' son but was his disciple; what Moses did not receive, Caleb did. So "God's promises cross generations." If this generation does not receive it, the next will. Hence the call to pass on excitement, expectation ("Lord, if you are willing") and a willingness to fight to our children, as his father passed his excitement on to him.

### Distinctions he draws
- **Excitement from inside out vs. self-hypnosis or outward cheerfulness:** "not from outside in, but from inside out" [1:07:52]; "not just self-hypnosis, positive, positive" [1:14:25]; the prayer contrasts outward "happiness" with inner excitement [1:33:12].
- **Excitement vs. personality:** not about being sanguine, extrovert or introvert [1:02:32], [1:06:29], [1:08:58].
- **Excitement over God's call vs. emotional reaction to opportunity:** "not just a city, not just a promotion" [1:04:15].
- **Following God vs. chasing dreams and signs:** no need for audible voices, prophecies or "pink sky" signs; follow God and the Bible [1:00:03] to [1:01:18].
- **Expectation vs. presumption:** expectation is not assuming God will fulfill every dream [1:25:57].
- **Expectation vs. mere optimism:** cheerful people without plans or schedules lack expectation [1:16:01].
- **Excitement/expectation vs. action:** nothing happens unless it is done [1:27:09], [1:27:44].
- **The right mountain vs. envy of others' blessings:** Caleb did not want Hermon or another valley; he was secure with Hebron; pursue what is truly your portion [1:27:50] to [1:28:59].
- **Material inheritance vs. spiritual inheritance:** "this and that" is fine, but pass on excitement in the Lord [1:29:43].
